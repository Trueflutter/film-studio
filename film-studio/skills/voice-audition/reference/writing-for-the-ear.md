# Writing for the ear

A script that reads well on a page often sounds stiff aloud. Write it to be heard.

- **Short sentences.** One idea each. If you run out of breath reading it, split it.
- **Contractions.** "It's", "you'll", "don't". People talk that way.
- **No ellipses.** "..." makes the voice hesitate and drag. End the sentence with a full stop.
- **No dashes.** Use a comma or a full stop. Voices read them oddly.
- **No lists of three nouns in a row** ("fast, simple, clear"). It sounds like a slogan. Say it as a sentence.
- **Numbers and names.** Write numbers the way you want them said ("twenty twenty-six", "ten seconds"). If a name is mispronounced, respell it as it sounds ("Saqr" as "Sa-ker") in the script copy only, and keep the real spelling for captions.
- **Emphasis.** Put the key word at the end of the sentence. Capitals shout; avoid them.
- **Stage directions.** Eleven v3 and v4 understand tags in square brackets such as `[softly]` or `[laughs]`. Use one now and then at the start of a line. They are not spoken, and the tools skip them when counting words. Test on the audition line first.
- **Read it aloud once** before spending credits.

## Length

Social and news pace is about 3 words per second. A 30 second video holds about 90 words. Count the words first, then write to fit.
